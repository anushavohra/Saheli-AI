CATEGORY GUIDANCE: JEWELRY

Pricing specifics for jewelry:
Jewelry pricing follows the same cost-plus formula as general pricing guidance, but materials require extra precision since components are often bought in bulk. Break down every component: beads, wire, findings (clasps, jump rings, earring hooks), and a share of packaging, calculated per piece, not per pack. A common mistake specific to jewelry: sellers price only the visible "hero" materials (main stone or bead) and forget small components like clasps and jump rings, which add up across many pieces. A standard approach: double your true cost for wholesale price, then double again for retail (4x total), though many sellers use 2-3x depending on market positioning.

Common jewelry-specific mistakes:
- Underpricing custom or made-to-order pieces the same as standard catalog items, custom work deserves a premium for the extra time and risk
- Not accounting for "findings" (small hardware components) as real costs, they're small individually but add up
- Overpricing based on personal attachment to a piece rather than actual cost and market rate

Photography note specific to jewelry:
Jewelry is a detail-heavy category, customers want to see texture, shine, and scale clearly. Close-up shots matter more here than in most categories, and accurate color representation is especially important since jewelry buyers often shop by exact color/finish (gold vs rose gold vs silver).

Know your customer type:
Jewelry buyers vary a lot in what they want, delicate everyday pieces, bold statement pieces, or gifting-occasion pieces (birthdays, weddings, anniversaries). Understanding which of these your shop leans toward helps you write more targeted descriptions and choose more relevant photos, rather than generic ones.
