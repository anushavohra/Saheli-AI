CATEGORY GUIDANCE: CROCHET & KNITTING

Pricing specifics for crochet and knitting:
Never use the "materials × 3" shortcut. This method wildly undervalues your time, a hat with Rs. 500 in yarn priced at Rs. 1,500 might leave you earning far below a fair hourly wage once the actual hours are counted. This is the single most common and damaging pricing mistake in this category.

The correct formula:
(Materials × 2) + (Hourly Wage × Hours Worked) = Price
Or the fuller version: Price = (Materials + Labor + Overhead) × Profit Multiplier

Track your time honestly:
Crochet and knitting work often happens in short bursts, during TV time, waiting somewhere, between other tasks, which makes it easy to underestimate total hours. Actually track your time per project rather than guessing, most sellers in this category underestimate hours by a wide margin, and labor typically makes up 60-80% of a fair price for handmade fiber items, meaning materials alone are only a small fraction of what you should actually charge.

Overhead items specific to this category, often forgotten:
Tissue paper/packaging (Rs. 50-150 per item), a share of your electricity bill (from lighting/working hours), and tool wear, hooks, needles, and yarn winders do wear out over time and are a real, if small, cost.

Know which items are worth selling:
Large, time-intensive items (blankets, especially) can take 40+ hours and require a very specific customer willing to pay accordingly, many sellers choose not to stock these regularly for this reason. Smaller items, hats, headbands, mug cozies, scarves, are more practical to keep in regular stock since they require less time and yarn per piece.

Premium materials justify higher prices:
Using higher-quality yarn (merino wool, alpaca, hand-dyed fibers) genuinely costs more and can justify a noticeably higher price, this isn't overpricing, it's accurately reflecting a real cost difference, and it also positions your work differently than mass-produced alternatives.

Customers often compare you to mass-produced items, don't let that set your price:
A customer may compare your handmade blanket to a Rs. 3,000 factory-made throw without realizing yours took 40+ hours of skilled handwork. This comparison isn't fair or accurate, factory items are machine-made at scale; crochet specifically cannot be machine-replicated at all, every piece is genuinely hand-worked. Don't let an unfair comparison talk you into underpricing.

Custom orders:
Always clarify time expectations upfront for custom pieces, and consider taking partial payment in advance, since custom fiber work represents a real time commitment before you even know the order is final.
