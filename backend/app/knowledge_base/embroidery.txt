CATEGORY GUIDANCE: EMBROIDERY

Pricing specifics for hand embroidery:
Hand embroidery is uniquely time-intensive, which means labor cost matters more here than in almost any other handmade category, and it's the cost sellers most often underestimate. Two approaches:

1. Stitch/time-based method: track how long a piece actually takes to complete, then multiply by a fair hourly rate for skilled handwork. Larger, more detailed pieces (like a fully embroidered dupatta or jacket panel) can easily take 8-10+ hours, be honest with yourself about real time spent, not an optimistic guess.

2. Simple cost-plus method: add materials (fabric, embroidery thread/floss, backing) + labor (hours × your hourly rate) + a small amount for tools/equipment wear, then apply your markup.

Whichever method you use, the core mistake to avoid is the same: pricing embroidery close to machine-embroidered or printed alternatives. Hand embroidery takes dramatically longer than machine work and represents a real skill, pricing it like a mass-produced alternative undervalues the actual time and craftsmanship involved.

Custom work deserves a clear premium:
If you take custom orders (a customer's own design or fabric), charge more than your standard catalog pieces, custom work requires extra communication time, and if you're creating a new design specifically for them, that design time should be priced in separately from the stitching itself.

Be realistic with delivery timelines:
Because hand embroidery takes real time, avoid promising faster delivery than you can comfortably deliver. Customers respond better to a slightly longer but reliable timeline than a rushed promise that gets delayed, this builds trust for repeat orders.

Photography note specific to embroidery:
Since the value of embroidery lies in fine detail and stitch quality, close-up shots are especially important here, more than almost any other category. A single far-away photo won't show a customer the actual craftsmanship they're paying for. Show at least one close-up per listing that clearly captures stitch texture and pattern detail.
