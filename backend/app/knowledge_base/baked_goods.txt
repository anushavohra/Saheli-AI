CATEGORY GUIDANCE: BAKED GOODS

Pricing specifics for baked goods:
Home bakers commonly undercharge, industry data suggests underpricing by 30-60% is a widespread pattern among new home bakers. A simple starting formula specific to baked goods: (ingredient cost + packaging cost) × 3 = minimum retail price. This multiplier is higher than typical handmade goods because ingredient costs are relatively low but labor, skill, and perishability risk are high.

Specialty ingredients justify higher prices:
If you use premium ingredients (specialty flour, real butter, high-quality vanilla, allergen-free ingredients), your costs are genuinely higher, and your price should reflect that clearly, don't absorb the extra cost to stay "affordable." Customers seeking specialty or allergen-friendly baked goods (like gluten-free) generally expect and accept a price premium, often 20-40% above standard baked goods, because they understand the ingredients and effort cost more.

Food safety matters more here than other categories:
Unlike jewelry or embroidery, food carries real health risk if handled poorly. Basic practices that protect both customers and your business: wash hands thoroughly before starting, use clean tools and surfaces for each batch, store ingredients in sealed containers, and keep the kitchen area free of pets during preparation. Always clearly label ingredients, especially common allergens (nuts, dairy, eggs, gluten), a customer needs to know what's in your product before buying, this protects them and protects you from disputes later.

Shelf life and packaging:
Baked goods are perishable, be upfront with customers about how fresh the item is and how long it will realistically stay good. Packaging matters more here too, it needs to protect freshness, not just look nice, since a customer's first bite is what determines whether they order again.

Keep your menu focused:
A smaller menu of 5-8 items you can make consistently and well tends to perform better than a large, scattered menu, both for quality control and for how easy it is for customers to decide what to order. Consistency in taste and quality is what turns a one-time buyer into a repeat customer.

Repeat customers matter more than reach:
For baked goods specifically, word-of-mouth and repeat orders tend to be a more reliable source of steady income than one-off social media attention. Prioritize consistent quality and reliable ordering over chasing viral reach.
