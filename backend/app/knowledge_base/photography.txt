PRODUCT PHOTOGRAPHY GUIDANCE

Why this matters more than most sellers realize:
Product photos are consistently one of the highest-impact factors in whether someone buys. Multiple independent studies show professional-quality images improve conversion rates by roughly 10-33%, compared to low-quality or amateur photos. Separately, 22% of all online product returns happen simply because the item looked different in person than in photos, meaning poor photos don't just lose sales, they actively cause returns and refunds later. Over 90% of online buyers say product photos are essential to their purchase decision, often ranked above the written description itself.

Lighting (the single biggest factor):
Use natural daylight from a window rather than indoor bulbs or flash, both tend to distort color accuracy and create harsh, unflattering shadows. Position your product about 3-4 feet from a large window. If direct sunlight is too harsh, diffuse it by hanging a white sheet or cloth over the window. If shadows on one side are too dark, place a plain white board or paper on the opposite side to bounce light back, this is a free version of the reflectors professional photographers pay for.

Resolution and technical baseline:
Where possible, shoot at your phone's highest resolution setting. Industry guidance recommends a minimum of 1500 pixels on the longest edge for product images, this ensures the image stays sharp even when a customer zooms in, which many will do before buying, especially for detail-heavy items like jewelry or embroidery.

Background:
Use a plain, clean, consistent background, a white sheet, poster board, or plain cloth works well. Keeps customer attention on the product and makes your shop look more professional and consistent.

Number of images matters:
Include multiple images per product, not just one. Show close-up detail shots (especially important for handmade items where craftsmanship and texture are part of the value), and different angles. For clothing, jewelry, and similarly detail-sensitive categories, more angles directly correlate with fewer returns, since customers can judge scale, texture, and true color before buying rather than guessing.

Accuracy prevents returns and lost repeat customers:
Since roughly 1 in 5 returns happen purely because a product "looked different than expected," treat accurate photos as a way to protect your profit, not just a way to attract a sale. A customer who returns an item due to a misleading photo often won't buy from you again either, in surveys, roughly 1 in 4 shoppers say they won't repurchase from a brand after a single bad return experience.

Consistency:
Use the same lighting setup and background style across all your product photos. A consistent visual style is one of the fastest ways to make a small home-based shop look established and trustworthy, even without a big budget.
