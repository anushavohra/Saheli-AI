CATEGORY GUIDANCE: CLAY CRAFTS (Pottery, Ceramics & Polymer Clay)

Pricing specifics for clay crafts:
Standard formula: Materials + Labor + Overhead = Cost, then Cost × 2 = Wholesale, Wholesale × 2 = Retail (roughly a 4x total markup on true cost). A fair labor rate for skilled clay work is commonly benchmarked at Rs. 700-2000+/hour depending on skill level and local market, don't undervalue your time just because the process feels enjoyable or routine to you.

Costs specific to this category, often forgotten:
- Firing costs: if using a kiln, energy use, element wear, and depreciation are real per-firing costs, even if the kiln itself is already paid off, "it's already mine" doesn't mean firing is free
- Breakage: pottery and ceramics have real breakage/loss rates during making or firing. If you lose roughly 10% of pieces to cracks or breaks and don't account for that in pricing, you're quietly absorbing that loss on every surviving piece
- "In-between" labor: loading/unloading, mixing glaze, sanding, finishing touches, these small tasks add up to real unpaid hours if not tracked, don't price only the visible shaping/sculpting time

For polymer clay specifically (no kiln needed, oven-baked):
Costs are simpler, primarily clay, any embellishments (metallic accents, hardware for earrings/jewelry), and baking time/electricity. Polymer clay is commonly used for smaller items like earrings and small decorative pieces, and pairs well with the jewelry pricing approach for finished pieces like earrings, materials + labor + overhead, then a 2-3x markup.

Never compare your handmade piece to mass-produced items:
A handmade mug is not the same product as a factory-made mug from a general store, different process, different value, different customer. Comparing your price to a cheap factory item and feeling pressure to match it undervalues genuinely different work.

One-of-a-kind pieces deserve a premium:
If a piece is truly unique, not a repeated design, it's reasonable to price it higher than a simple, repeatable form. Uniqueness is a legitimate value factor customers do pay for in handmade goods.

Revisit pricing periodically:
Material costs (clay, glaze) and utility costs change over time, prices set even a year ago may no longer reflect real costs, revisit and adjust periodically rather than assuming your original calculation stays accurate indefinitely.
